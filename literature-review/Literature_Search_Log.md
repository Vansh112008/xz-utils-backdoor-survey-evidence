## 4. Search-result counts and limitations

The discovery interface returned source pages and excerpts but did not provide a stable, reproducible database hit count for each query. Accordingly, this log does **not** claim Google Scholar, IEEE Xplore or ACM result totals. A search-engine result count is also not equivalent to the number of unique studies screened or included.

The source records were checked individually where publisher or institutional metadata was available. Publication dates are recorded in the literature matrix and reference list. Where a record could not be independently confirmed, it should be marked for further verification rather than assigned a fabricated date.

## 5. Inclusion criteria

Sources were retained when they:
1. Addressed CVE-2024-3094 or the XZ Utils incident; or
2. Examined open-source supply-chain security, contributor practices, build integrity, dependency risks, SBOM accuracy, reproducible builds or CI/CD security; and
3. Provided identifiable bibliographic or institutional metadata; and
4. Contributed technical, empirical, methodological or authoritative guidance relevant to the review questions.

Peer-reviewed papers, government guidance, incident disclosures, advisories and preprints are distinguished by publication type. A preprint is not represented as peer reviewed solely because it is hosted on arXiv.

## 6. Exclusion and deduplication

Duplicate records and duplicate versions were not counted as separate works. The literature matrix and BibTeX library were reconciled so that each of the 25 numbered entries maps to a distinct bibliography record. A previously duplicated XZ Utils paper entry was removed from the matrix and replaced with a distinct source already present in the bibliography.

## 8. Limitations

The original student search history and hit counts were not retained. This verification pass therefore improves the traceability of the final source set but cannot reconstruct earlier searches. The review is a structured literature survey with transparent source verification, not a claim of a fully reproducible systematic review with complete historical database screening counts.
